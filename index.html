The whole interaction

Three moves, then it’s in your document

  1. 01

    Hold the key

    Press and hold your push-to-talk key — Fn or ⌃ Space. A quiet overlay appears at the bottom of the screen.

  2. 02

    Speak naturally

    Say your sentence. Your words stream into the overlay as you talk, with filler words and stray punctuation cleaned up on the fly.

  3. 03

    Release to insert

    Let go and the finished text is typed into the focused app. Changed your mind mid-sentence? Press Esc to cancel.

What you get

Built for everyday dictation, tuned for control

Smart formatting

Capitalization, punctuation, and filler removal are on by default. Say “new line” or “comma” and it does the right thing.

Custom vocabulary

Teach it your names and jargon, and add “heard → correct” fixes so “clod code” always lands as “Claude Code.”

Built-in offline AI

Refine your text with a small AI model that runs entirely on your Mac — no setup, no server, nothing leaves the machine. Or point it at your own server or OpenAI.

Refine while you dictate

Want what you’re saying reworked? Keep holding, tap the Refine key, and say “make it a Telegram post” — on release, the AI rewrites it before it lands.

WhisperKit by default

Transcription runs on WhisperKit — Whisper on Apple’s Neural Engine via CoreML — for fast, real-time streaming. whisper.cpp and Apple Speech stay available as fallbacks, one click away.

Transcription history

A local, searchable list of everything you’ve dictated, ready to copy or reuse. Stored on your machine, never uploaded.

Twelve languages

Pick a language or let it auto-detect, with optional translate-to-English. Models range from tiny to large-v3.

Per-app modes

Set the language, output style, and cleanup per application, so your terminal and your email client behave differently.

New · voice editing

Select text anywhere, then edit it by voice

OpenWhisp isn’t just for dictating new text. Highlight a sentence in any app, hold your dictation key, tap the Refine key — right ⌃ by default — and say what you want changed. Release, and it’s rewritten in place. No retyping, no copy-paste, no switching windows.

  • “Make it more formal” · “Translate to Russian” · “Tighten this up” — plain language, any language.
  • Your selection is read through the Accessibility API — and if an app doesn’t expose it, your clipboard is restored after the fallback copy. Secure and password fields are never read.
  • The rewrite can run on the built-in offline model or your own local server to stay fully private, or OpenAI if you prefer.

Agent Bridge · built-in MCP server

Give your coding agent a voice — yours

OpenWhisp doubles as a local MCP server and command-line tool for Claude Code, Cursor, Hermes, and OpenClaw. Instead of stopping mid-task to wait for typed input, your agent asks you out loud — you answer by voice and it keeps working. No other dictation app ships this.

  • openwhisp_dictate

    Your agent pauses and asks you a question by voice. The overlay opens, you speak, and the moment you go quiet the transcript returns to the agent — no typing paragraphs into a chat box.

  • openwhisp_refineonly in OpenWhisp

    Agents rewrite text with the same on-device model as your refine hotkey — your prompts, your machine, nothing sent anywhere.

  • openwhisp_history

    Recent dictations — text, timestamp, target app — readable by the agent, stored only on your Mac.

# one-time setup — registers the server + agent instructions
openwhisp setup claude-code

# later, mid-task, your agent asks out loud:
● openwhisp_dictate("Deploy to staging or production?")
  ↳ overlay opens · you speak · silence sends it
● "production, and tag it v1.4"

# the CLI composes in pipelines, too
pbpaste | openwhisp refine -i "make it formal" | pbcopy

Off by default. Nothing listens until you enable it in Settings, and you approve each agent the first time it connects. The bridge is a private socket on your Mac — no open port, no cloud, only signed clients — agents can’t touch cloud AI unless you allow it, and pressing your dictation hotkey always takes the mic back from an agent.

Privacy by construction

The default is “stays on this Mac”

  • Audio is recorded locally and the recording is deleted after each transcription.
  • History, settings, and any API keys live on your machine — keys in the macOS Keychain, not in plain text.
  • AI cleanup can run fully on-device: the built-in offline model and your own local server both keep everything on your Mac. The only time text leaves your machine is if you choose the OpenAI provider.
  • Your transcript text is never written to the app’s log files.

Private AI · runs on your hard drive

The AI lives on your Mac, not in someone’s cloud

Turn on AI cleanup and OpenWhisp downloads a small open-weights model that runs entirely on your own machine — no API key, no account, no server to spin up. The weights sit in a folder on your disk, the model runs there, and your text never leaves the computer. Pick the one that fits your speed-versus-quality taste:

  • Qwen2.5 0.5BDefault

    Fastest, lowest memory. The default.

    491 MB|Apache-2.0
  • Qwen2.5 1.5B

    Higher quality, a little heavier.

    1.12 GB|Apache-2.0
  • SmolLM2 360M

    Smallest footprint, for tight RAM.

    271 MB|Apache-2.0

All three are Apache-2.0 licensed. The model downloads once, like the speech models, then works offline. Default is off — you opt in. Prefer your own setup? Point it at a local server instead, or at OpenAI.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Does my audio ever leave my Mac?

No. Transcription runs entirely on your Mac and works offline. Audio is never uploaded, and the recording is deleted after each transcription. The only time any text leaves your machine is if you explicitly choose the optional OpenAI provider for AI cleanup.

How much does OpenWhisp cost?

Nothing. OpenWhisp is free and open source under the MIT license — no account, no subscription, no usage meter.

What are the system requirements?

An Apple Silicon Mac (M1 or newer) running macOS 14 Sonoma or later.

How is it different from the built-in macOS dictation?

OpenWhisp runs Whisper-class models for noticeably better accuracy, and adds things macOS dictation doesn’t have: voice editing of selected text, AI refinement that runs offline, custom vocabulary, per-app modes, and a searchable local history.

How do I install OpenWhisp?

Download the DMG, drag OpenWhisp to your Applications folder, and open it. The build is signed and notarized by Apple, so it launches with a double-click — no Gatekeeper prompts. You can also build it yourself from source.
